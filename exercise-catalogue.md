# Calisthenics Arc — Controlled Bodyweight Exercise Catalogue

**Research snapshot:** 9 November 2026  
**Catalogue entries:** 277 named entries and meaningful variations across 13 sections.  
**Unique names after exact-name cleanup:** 277.  
**Purpose:** a searchable starting index for the Calisthenics Arc journal — not a workout, challenge, technique manual, or claim that every exercise ever invented is listed.

## Scope and limits

This catalogue focuses on controlled bodyweight strength, trunk control, ordinary locomotion, balance, and mobility movements. It includes common variations where the movement or setup meaningfully changes.

No web search can prove that it found every exercise, every regional name, every private app/database, or every website. Variants are effectively open-ended: a push-up can be changed by angle, stance, range, tempo, pause, surface, or assistance. The count above is a count of catalogue rows, not a count of every possible permutation or a universal total of calisthenics exercises.

High-risk acrobatic or extreme-leverage skills, maximal one-arm/finger-loading variations, kipping/explosive stunts, partner stunts, and other coach-only skill work are intentionally outside this catalogue. They are not DIY challenges and are not given progressions here.

## Equipment key

- **[F]** Floor / no equipment.
- **[W]** Wall.
- **[C]** A low, stable, non-slip step or chair used only as a support/step where labelled. Do not use furniture as a hanging or rowing anchor.
- **[B]** A purpose-built, correctly installed fixed pull-up bar.
- **[P]** Secure purpose-built parallel bars.
- **[R]** Properly installed gymnastics rings or suspension equipment.
- **[T]** A towel or slider on a suitable floor.

The repository's current `materials.md` record says a pull-up bar is unavailable. Therefore [B], [P], and [R] entries are reference-only until the correct equipment is available and an adult/qualified coach has checked the setup. Never improvise a hang, row, or dip station from a window grill, door, table, bed, or loose chairs.

## Variation dimensions (not separate exercise names)

Many libraries multiply their counts by listing each combination as a separate item. To keep this catalogue readable, the following are treated as ways to vary an existing exercise, not as entirely new movements:

- **Body angle:** more upright, more horizontal, incline, or decline.
- **Support and range:** supported/assisted, partial range, comfortable full range, or a pause.
- **Tempo:** controlled lowering, controlled lifting, or a deliberate pause.
- **Stance:** narrow, standard, wide, split stance, or staggered stance.
- **Side:** bilateral, alternating, or one side at a time when appropriate.
- **Lever:** bent-knee versus straight-leg versions where suitable.
- **Contraction:** moving repetitions versus a controlled static hold.
- **Surface/equipment:** floor, wall, stable step, fixed bar, parallel bars, or properly installed rings.

A variation should get its own catalogue row only when it has a distinct name or a meaningful change in movement, leverage, or equipment.

## Safety and use

This is an index, not a prescription. It gives no target reps, sets, maximal holds, or progression deadlines. Choose exercises only with sound technique, a suitable environment, and age-appropriate instruction. The American Academy of Pediatrics states that youth resistance training can include bodyweight movements and can have low injury rates when technique is emphasized and sessions are well supervised; its clinical report was reaffirmed in November 2024. See the research audit for the source.

Stop rather than train through pain, and do not turn catalogue entries into contests or max-effort tests. For a young athlete, ask a parent/guardian and a qualified coach to review equipment-based or more demanding movements. Use only fixed, purpose-built equipment and keep the training area clear.

## Maintenance rules

1. Add a new row only when the name is distinct or the movement/setup is meaningfully different.
2. Record common aliases beside the canonical name rather than counting them twice.
3. Tag equipment honestly; never imply that unavailable equipment exists.
4. Keep instruction, progressions, and training volume in separate reviewed documents.
5. Re-run the safety and duplicate checks before adding advanced entries.
